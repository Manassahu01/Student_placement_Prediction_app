How a prediction is made

  1. Describe the student

    Fill in ten details: branch, daily habits, attendance and scores. No names or IDs are needed.

  2. The tree asks questions

    At each step the model compares one value with a threshold it learned from past students, then follows a branch.

  3. Read the probability

    The path ends at a leaf. The share of placed students in that leaf is the probability you see.

Under the hood

Model
A decision tree classifier from scikit-learn. Tree depth, minimum leaf size and split rule were tuned with a randomized search and 5-fold cross-validation, then checked on a held-out 20% of the data.
Training data
About 10,000 student records, roughly half of them placed. Duplicates were dropped, branch names cleaned up, daily hours capped to sensible ranges and missing values filled with medians.
Inputs
Ten inputs become 16 model features, because branch is split into one column per branch plus an unknown group.
Why results move in steps
The probability is the share of placed students in a single leaf, so it jumps between values instead of changing smoothly. A small change can leave it unchanged until a threshold is crossed.
